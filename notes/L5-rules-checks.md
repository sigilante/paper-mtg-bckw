## Q1. Copies of spells and targets

**Verdict: yes on all sub-questions, with one condition: a copy may be retargeted to a different kind of object exactly when the spell's own targeting text allows that kind; a spell cannot target itself; all-targets-illegal fizzles, some-illegal is partial.**

CR 707.10: "To copy a spell, activated ability, or triggered ability means to put a copy of it onto the stack; a copy of a spell isn't cast and a copy of an activated ability isn't activated. A copy of a spell or ability copies both the characteristics of the spell or ability and all decisions made for it, including modes, targets, the value of X, and additional or alternative costs. (See rule 601, "Casting Spells.") Choices that are normally made on resolution are not copied. ... A copy of a spell is owned by the player under whose control it was put on the stack. A copy of a spell or ability is controlled by the player under whose control it was put on the stack. A copy of a spell is itself a spell, even though it has no spell card associated with it. ..."
(Its Fork example: "It does not necessarily have the same target, but only because Fork allows choosing of new targets.")

CR 707.10c: "Some effects copy a spell or ability and state that its controller may choose new targets for the copy. The player may leave any number of the targets unchanged, even if those targets would be illegal. If the player chooses to change some or all of the targets, the new targets must be legal. Once the player has decided what the copy's targets will be, the copy is put onto the stack with those targets."

CR 115.7d (same idea for "choose new targets"): "If an effect allows a player to "choose new targets" for a spell or ability, the player may leave any number of the targets unchanged, even if those targets would be illegal. If the player chooses to change some or all of the targets, the new targets must be legal and must not cause any unchanged targets to become illegal."

Kind of object. "Legal" means legal under the copy's own targeting text. CR 115.2: "Only permanents are legal targets for spells and abilities, unless a spell or ability (a) specifies that it can target an object in another zone or a player, or (b) targets an object that can't exist on the battlefield, such as a spell or ability." So: a copy of a spell whose text says "target spell or permanent" may be retargeted from a permanent to a spell (or vice versa), because both are legal for that text. A copy of a spell that says only "target creature" may not. There is no rule that forces the new target to be the same kind; the only constraint is legality against the text. Real example text: Aether Gust, "Choose target spell or permanent that's red or green. Its owner puts it on their choice of the top or bottom of their library." (Below, Q2.)

Same targets kept? Yes, by default (707.10: targets are among the "decisions made" that are copied). A copy with "new targets" may leave any number unchanged (707.10c). Also CR 115.3: "The same target can't be chosen multiple times for any one instance of the word "target" on a spell or ability. If the spell or ability uses the word "target" in multiple places, the same object or player can be chosen once for each instance of the word "target" (as long as it fits the targeting criteria). This rule applies both when choosing targets for a spell or ability and when changing targets or choosing new targets ..."
Change-vs-choose-new: CR 115.7a: "If an effect allows a player to "change the target(s)" of a spell or ability, each target can be changed only to another legal target. If a target can't be changed to another legal target, the original target is unchanged, even if the original target is itself illegal by then. If all the targets aren't changed to other legal targets, none of them are changed." 115.7e: "When changing targets or choosing new targets for a spell or ability, only the final set of targets is evaluated to determine whether the change is legal." (Its example is Arc Trail + Redirect: targets can be swapped with each other.) 115.7f: the division of damage/counters can't be changed. 115.8: modes can't be changed.

Self-target. CR 115.5: "A spell or ability on the stack is an illegal target for itself." (Note: this is about the object itself. A copy is a different object, so, e.g., a copy could legally target the original if the text allows; I did not find a rule or ruling either way beyond 115.5: UNVERIFIED inference.)

Resolution with illegal targets. CR 608.2b (full relevant text): "If the spell or ability specifies targets, it checks whether the targets are still legal. A target that's no longer in the zone it was in when it was targeted is illegal. Other changes to the game state may cause a target to no longer be legal; ... If all its targets, for every instance of the word "target," are now illegal, the spell or ability doesn't resolve. It's removed from the stack and, if it's a spell, put into its owner's graveyard. Otherwise, the spell or ability will resolve normally. Illegal targets, if any, won't be affected by parts of a resolving spell's effect for which they're illegal. Other parts of the effect for which those targets are not illegal may still affect them. ... If part of the effect requires information about an illegal target, it fails to determine any such information. Any part of the effect that requires that information won't happen." (Examples there: Sorin's Thirst; Plague Spores.) Also 707.10a (copy ceases to exist off the stack) is in Q8.
Note: "removed from the stack and, if it's a spell, put into its owner's graveyard" — a copy that fizzles is then in a zone other than the stack and ceases to exist (707.10a).

## Q3. Splice onto Arcane

**Verdict: partly. By the rule text, spliced text is a text-changing effect and is therefore NOT copied; a copy cannot be spliced onto; each card can be spliced onto a given spell only once. I found no official ruling that says anything about copying a spliced spell (my scan of rulings on 27 splice-Arcane cards for "copy" returned nothing).**

CR 702.47a: "Splice is a static ability that functions while a card is in your hand. "Splice onto [quality] [cost]" means "You may reveal this card from your hand as you cast a [quality] spell. If you do, that spell gains the text of this card's rules text and you pay [cost] as an additional cost to cast that spell." ..."
CR 702.47b: "You can't choose to use a splice ability if you can't make the required choices (targets, etc.) for that card's rules text. You can't splice any one card onto the same spell more than once. If you're splicing more than one card onto a spell, reveal them all at once and choose the order in which their effects will happen. The effects of the main spell must happen first."
CR 702.47c: "The spell has the characteristics of the main spell, plus the rules text of each of the spliced cards. This is a text-changing effect (see rule 612, "Text-Changing Effects"). The spell doesn't gain any other characteristics (name, mana cost, color, supertypes, card types, subtypes, etc.) of the spliced cards. ..."
CR 702.47d: "Choose targets for the added text normally (see rule 601.2c). Note that a spell with one or more targets won't resolve if all of its targets are illegal on resolution."
CR 702.47e: "The spell loses any splice changes once it leaves the stack for any reason."
CR 612.10: "A splice ability changes a spell's text by adding the rules text of the card with splice to the spell, following that spell's own rules text. It doesn't modify or replace any of that spell's own text. (See rule 702.47, "Splice.")"
CR 601.2b (splicing happens at casting): "If the player wishes to splice any cards onto the spell (see rule 702.47), they reveal those cards in their hand."
CR 707.2: "The copiable values are the values derived from the text printed on the object (...), as modified by other copy effects, by its face-down status, and by "as . . . enters" and "as . . . is turned face up" abilities that set power and toughness (and may also set additional characteristics). Other effects (including type-changing and text-changing effects), status, counters, and stickers are not copied."
Reading: 707.2 excludes text-changing effects from copiable values; 702.47c says splice is a text-changing effect; so a copy (e.g. from Twincast) of a spliced spell does not carry the spliced text (my inference from two quoted rules; no ruling found). One wrinkle: 707.10 copies "all decisions made for it, including ... targets", so the copy keeps the original's chosen targets list, but the spliced text that would use some of them is not copied (UNVERIFIED how this plays out; I did not find a ruling).
Can a copy be spliced onto? Splice is a choice made "as you cast" (702.47a, 601.2b) and "a copy of a spell isn't cast" (707.10), so no. (Inference from quoted text.)
Repeat limits: only "any one card ... onto the same spell more than once" is forbidden (702.47b). Ruling on Glacial Ray (2013-06-07): "You reveal all cards you intend to splice at the same time. Each individual card can be spliced only once onto any one spell." Also: "A card with a splice ability can't be spliced onto itself because the spell is on the stack (and not in your hand) when you reveal the cards you want to splice onto it." Two physical cards with the same name are separate cards, so by the quoted wording both may be spliced (inference; no ruling quoted).
Oracle: Glacial Ray {1}{R} Instant — Arcane: "Glacial Ray deals 2 damage to any target. / Splice onto Arcane {1}{R} (As you cast an Arcane spell, you may reveal this card from your hand and pay its splice cost. If you do, add this card's effects to that spell.)" Evermind (no mana cost) Instant — Arcane: "(Nonexistent mana costs can't be paid.) / Draw a card. / Splice onto Arcane {1}{U} (...)". Reach Through Mists {U} Instant — Arcane: "Draw a card."
Consequence for the Poet's idea 4: a spliced word is not copied by Twincast per 707.2 + 702.47c (inference), and each card splices once.

## Q4. Mutate / merged permanents

**Verdict: partly. The rules fix what a merged permanent is and what happens when it leaves; what a copy of a merged permanent or of a mutating spell becomes is only partly written. I found no explicit CR line or ruling on either; the answers below are my reading of 731.2a and 707.10 and are marked.**

CR 702.140a: "Mutate appears on some creature cards. It represents a static ability that functions while the spell with mutate is on the stack. "Mutate [cost]" means "You may pay [cost] rather than pay this spell's mana cost. If you do, it becomes a mutating creature spell and targets a non-Human creature with the same owner as this spell."..."
CR 702.140b: "As a mutating creature spell begins resolving, if its target is illegal, it ceases to be a mutating creature spell and continues resolving as a creature spell and will be put onto the battlefield under the control of the spell's controller."
CR 702.140c: "As a mutating creature spell resolves, if its target is legal, it doesn't enter the battlefield. Rather, it merges with the target creature and becomes one object represented by more than one card or token (see rule 731, "Merging with Permanents"). The spell's controller chooses whether the spell is put on top of the creature or on the bottom. The resulting permanent is a mutated permanent."
CR 702.140e: "A mutated permanent has all abilities of each card and token that represents it. Its other characteristics are derived from the topmost card or token."
CR 731.2: "To merge an object with a permanent, place that object on top of or under that permanent. That permanent becomes a merged permanent represented by the card or copy that represented that object in addition to any other components that were representing it."
CR 731.2a: "A merged permanent has only the characteristics of its topmost component, unless otherwise specified by the effect that caused them to merge. This is a copiable effect whose timestamp is the time the objects merged. (See rule 613.2.)"
CR 731.2b: "As an object merges with a permanent, that object leaves its previous zone and becomes part of an object on the battlefield, but the resulting permanent isn't considered to have just entered the battlefield."
CR 731.2d: "If a merged permanent contains a token, the resulting permanent is a token only if the topmost component is a token."
CR 608.3d: "If the object that's resolving is a mutating creature spell, the object representing that spell merges with the permanent it is targeting (see rule 731, "Merging with Permanents")."

Token copy of a merged permanent. 731.2a says the merge is "a copiable effect" and gives the permanent "only the characteristics of its topmost component, unless otherwise specified by the effect that caused them to merge"; 702.140e specifies that the mutated permanent has all abilities of every component. So my reading: a copy gets the topmost component's copiable values plus all component abilities (because the mutate effect is itself a copiable effect). UNVERIFIED: no ruling or rule line found that states this outright for token copies or Clone effects. The only mutate-related copy statement I found (from mtg.wiki Mutate page, https://mtg.wiki/page/Mutate): "If a creature that's a copy of another creature mutates, the characteristics it gains from mutating apply after applying the copy effect." Also the copy is not itself merged (it's a new single object), so it has no stack of components (inference).

Copy of a mutating creature spell. 707.10 copies "all decisions made for it, including modes, targets, the value of X, and additional or alternative costs," and mutate is an alternative cost that makes it "a mutating creature spell" that "targets a non-Human creature with the same owner as this spell" (702.140a). My reading: the copy is a mutating creature spell with the same target (or new if allowed), and on resolving a copy of a permanent spell "ceases being a copy of a spell and becomes a token permanent" (707.10f). Then, if its target is legal, it merges (608.3d) and the resulting merged permanent is a token only if the topmost component is a token (731.2d). UNVERIFIED: I found no official ruling on this exact interaction (Scryfall rulings on Gemrazer, Dirge Bat, Nethroi, Vulpikeet, Brokkos did not mention copying a mutating spell).
Also 707.10f: "Some effects copy a permanent spell. As that copy resolves, it ceases being a copy of a spell and becomes a token permanent. (See rule 608.3f.)"

Leaves the battlefield. CR 731.3: "If a merged permanent leaves the battlefield, one permanent leaves the battlefield and each of the individual components are put into the appropriate zone." 731.3a: "If a merged permanent is put into its owner's graveyard or library, that player may arrange the new objects in any order. If it's put into its owner's library, that player doesn't reveal the order." 731.3c: "If an effect can find the new object that a merged permanent becomes as it leaves the battlefield, it finds all of those objects. ... the same actions are taken upon each of them." 731.3d: replacement effects applied to the event apply to all components.

Oracle (mutate): Gemrazer {3}{G} Creature — Beast: "Mutate {1}{G}{G} (If you cast this spell for its mutate cost, put it over or under target non-Human creature you own. They mutate into the creature on top plus all abilities from under it.) / Reach, trample / Whenever this creature mutates, destroy target artifact or enchantment an opponent controls." (Others checked only for rulings.)

## Q5. Phasing (702.26)

**Verdict: yes, all four points are explicit in the CR.**

CR 702.26a: "Phasing is a static ability that modifies the rules of the untap step. During each player's untap step, before the active player untaps permanents, all phased-in permanents with phasing that player controls "phase out." Simultaneously, all phased-out permanents that had phased out under that player's control "phase in.""
CR 702.26b: "If a permanent phases out, its status changes to "phased out." Except for rules and effects that specifically mention phased-out permanents, a phased-out permanent is treated as though it does not exist. It can't affect or be affected by anything else in the game. A permanent that phases out is removed from combat. (See rule 506.4.) Example: You control three creatures, one of which is phased out. You cast a spell that says "Draw a card for each creature you control." You draw two cards. Example: You control a phased-out creature. You cast a spell that says "Destroy all creatures." The phased-out creature is not destroyed."
CR 702.26c: "If a permanent phases in, its status changes to "phased in." The game once again treats it as though it exists."
CR 702.26d: "The phasing event doesn't actually cause a permanent to change zones or control, even though it's treated as though it's not on the battlefield and not under its controller's control while it's phased out. Zone-change triggers don't trigger when a permanent phases in or out. Tokens continue to exist on the battlefield while phased out. Counters and stickers remain on a permanent while it's phased out. Effects that check a phased-in permanent's history won't treat the phasing event as having caused the permanent to leave or enter the battlefield or its controller's control."
CR 702.26e: continuous effects from spells/abilities that modify characteristics or change control "won't include" phased-out permanents (including ones that reference the permanent specifically, "unless they also specifically refer to the permanent as phased out").
CR 702.26f: "Continuous effects that affect a phased-out permanent may expire while that permanent is phased out. ... effects with "for as long as" durations that track that permanent (see rule 611.2b) end when that permanent phases out because they can no longer see it."
CR 702.26g (indirect phasing): "When a permanent phases out, any Auras, Equipment, or Fortifications attached to that permanent phase out at the same time. This alternate way of phasing out is known as phasing out "indirectly." An Aura, Equipment, or Fortification that phased out indirectly won't phase in by itself, but instead phases in along with the permanent it's attached to."
CR 702.26h: "If an object would simultaneously phase out directly and indirectly, it just phases out indirectly."
CR 702.26i: "An Aura, Equipment, or Fortification that phased out directly will phase in attached to the object or player it was attached to when it phased out, if that object is still in the same zone or that player is still in the game. If not, that Aura, Equipment, or Fortification phases in unattached. State-based actions apply as appropriate. (See rules 704.5m and 704.5n.)"
CR 702.26j: "Abilities that trigger when a permanent becomes attached or unattached from an object or player don't trigger when that permanent phases in or out."
Also 702.26k (phased-out permanents of a player who leaves the game leave the game), 702.26p ("Multiple instances of phasing on the same permanent are redundant.").
Counters on phasing in: counters remain (702.26d). Attachments on phasing in: see 702.26g/i. Note 702.26g says indirect phase-out recurses only to attachments (Auras/Equipment/Fortifications) of the permanent, which by 303.4/704.5m includes Auras attached to Auras.
Card examples: I did not look up phasing cards.

## Q6. Auras on Auras; copying with attachments; token Auras

**Verdict: (a) Auras can enchant Auras only via "Enchant enchantment"/"Enchant permanent"/"artifact or enchantment" style abilities; I found NO card with "Enchant Aura" (Scryfall search `o:"enchant Aura"` returned no results, HTTP 404 = zero matches). (b) Your belief is confirmed: I found no rule or effect that copies a permanent together with its attachments. (c) Token Auras choose their host per 303.4f.**

CR 303.4: "Some enchantments have the subtype "Aura." An Aura enters the battlefield attached to an object or player. What an Aura can be attached to is defined by its enchant keyword ability (see rule 702.5, "Enchant"). Other effects can limit what a permanent can be enchanted by."
CR 303.4c: "If an Aura is enchanting an illegal object or player as defined by its enchant ability and other applicable effects, the object it was attached to no longer exists, or the player it was attached to has left the game, the Aura is put into its owner's graveyard. (This is a state-based action. See rule 704.)"
CR 303.4d: "An Aura can't enchant itself. If this occurs somehow, the Aura is put into its owner's graveyard. An Aura that's also a creature can't enchant anything. If this occurs somehow, the Aura becomes unattached, then is put into its owner's graveyard. ... An Aura can't enchant more than one object or player. If a spell or ability would cause an Aura to become attached to more than one object or player, the Aura's controller chooses which object or player it becomes attached to."
CR 704.5m: "If an Aura is attached to an illegal object or player, or is not attached to an object or player, that Aura is put into its owner's graveyard."
CR 303.4f: "If an Aura is entering the battlefield under a player's control by any means other than by resolving as an Aura spell, and the effect putting it onto the battlefield doesn't specify the object or player the Aura will enchant, that player chooses what it will enchant as the Aura enters the battlefield. The player must choose a legal object or player according to the Aura's enchant ability and any other applicable effects."
CR 303.4g: "If an Aura is entering the battlefield and there is no legal object or player for it to enchant, the Aura remains in its current zone, unless that zone is the stack. In that case, the Aura is put into its owner's graveyard instead of entering the battlefield. If the Aura is a token, it isn't created."

CR 701.3d: "...If an Aura, Equipment, or Fortification that was attached to an object or player ceases to be attached to it, that counts as "becoming unattached [from that object or player]"; this includes if that Aura, Equipment, or Fortification leaves the battlefield, the object leaves the zone it was in, or that player leaves the game."

## Q7. Native binary relations between permanents

**Verdict: partly (list below is what I found in the CR; "complete" is not claimed). Plain asymmetric many-to-one links survive an endpoint leaving mainly by dissolving (attachment, pairing), by persisting as memory (exiled-with, haunt, champion), or by lingering as a combat state (blocked).**

For each: rule, symmetric?, cardinality, endpoint leaves.
1. **Attached / enchants / equips / fortifies** (CR 301.5, 301.6, 303.4, 701.3). Asymmetric (attachment -> host); each Aura/Equipment/Fortification has at most one host (303.4d: "An Aura can't enchant more than one object or player"; 301.5c: "An Equipment can't equip more than one creature."), a host may have many attachments (Roles: at most one per controller, 303.7a). Host may be a player (Aura only; 303.4b). Host leaves: Aura -> graveyard (303.4c, 704.5m); Equipment/Fortification -> unattached, stays (301.5c, 704.5n). Attachment leaves: host just has fewer. 701.3d: leaving counts as "becoming unattached". 303.4m/301.5f: "An ability of a permanent that refers to the "enchanted [object or player]" refers to whatever object or player that permanent is attached to, even if the permanent with the ability isn't an Aura." (So the relation is the generic "attached to".)
2. **Paired (soulbond)** (CR 702.95). SYMMETRIC; exactly one partner (702.95d: "A creature can be paired with only one other creature."). 702.95e: "A paired creature becomes unpaired if any of the following occur: another player gains control of it or the creature it's paired with; it or the creature it's paired with stops being a creature; or it or the creature it's paired with leaves the battlefield." So the survivor becomes unpaired. 702.95c: pairing fails on resolution if either is no longer a creature/on battlefield/under the right control.
3. **Blocking / blocked by** (CR 509.1g, 509.1h, 506.4, 510.1c-d). Asymmetric roles; many-to-many (a blocker may block several attackers with an ability; an attacker may have several blockers: "If two or more creatures are blocking it, it assigns its combat damage to those creatures divided as its controller chooses"). 509.1h: "A creature remains blocked even if all the creatures blocking it are removed from combat." 506.4: removal from combat if it "leaves the battlefield, if its controller or protector changes, if it phases out ...". Endpoint leaves: the attacker stays "blocked" and assigns no damage if no blockers remain (510.1c); a blocker that stops blocking assigns none (510.1d). Combat relations last only until end of combat.
4. **Attacking (creature -> player/planeswalker/battle)** (CR 508.1b, 506.3, 506.4c). Asymmetric; one target each. If the attacked planeswalker/battle leaves, the creature "continues to be an attacking creature, although it is not attacking any player, planeswalker, or battle ... If it is unblocked, it will deal no combat damage" (506.4c).
5. **Battle protector** (CR 310.9; battle -> player, not permanent-permanent): "Each battle has a player designated as its protector." 310.9f: "A battle can have only one protector at a time." Persists through copy changes (310.9g).
6. **Exiled with** (linked abilities, CR 607.2a, 607.3). Asymmetric: source object -> set of cards in exile; many cards per source. 607.2a: "If an object has an activated or triggered ability printed on it that instructs a player to exile one or more cards and an ability printed on it that refers either to "the exiled cards" or to cards "exiled with [this object]," these abilities are linked. The second ability refers only to cards in the exile zone that were put there as a result of an instruction to exile them in the first ability." Source leaving: by CR 400.7 a card/permanent that changes zones becomes a new object; I did not find in my greps what happens to the exiled-with set after the source leaves (UNVERIFIED; it depends on the card and 607.2's list).
7. **Created/put-onto-battlefield with** (CR 607.2c): "an ability printed on it that refers to objects "put onto the battlefield with [this object]" or "created with [this object]," those abilities are linked." Same shape as 6.
8. **Chosen value / noted info** (CR 607.2d, 607.2e): "the chosen [value]" refers "only to a choice made as a result of the first ability"; a relation between one permanent and a stored value, not between two permanents.
9. **Champion** (CR 702.72): "When this permanent enters, sacrifice it unless you exile another [object] you control" and "When this permanent leaves the battlefield, return the exiled card to the battlefield under its owner's control." 702.72c: "A permanent is "championed" by another permanent if the latter exiles the former as the direct result of a champion ability." Asymmetric; one championed object per champion; the champion leaving returns the championed card; the championed card is in exile (not a permanent) meanwhile. (Whether any championed card is currently printed: not checked.)
10. **Haunt** (CR 702.55a-c): "Cards that are in the exile zone as the result of a haunt ability "haunt" the creature targeted by that ability. The phrase "creature it haunts" refers to the object targeted by the haunt ability, regardless of whether or not that object is still a creature." and "Triggered abilities of cards with haunt that refer to the haunted creature can trigger in the exile zone." The haunting card is an exiled card (not a permanent) and haunted creature is a creature; endpoint "leaves": the exile-card keeps referring to it (the object after it left is a new object per 400.7; UNVERIFIED how that is handled).
11. **Goaded** (CR 701.15b): "Goaded is a designation a permanent can have. A goaded creature attacks each combat if able and attacks a player other than the controller of the permanent, spell, or ability that caused it to be goaded if able. Goaded is neither an ability nor part of the permanent's copiable values." Relation is permanent -> player(s) (701.15c: "A creature can be goaded by multiple players."), expires at the goader's next turn (701.15a). Not permanent-permanent.
12. **Crewed / saddled** (CR 702.122b-c, 702.171b-c): "A Vehicle is "crewed by" a creature if that creature was tapped to pay the cost to activate that Vehicle's crew ability." Historical record only; crew is "until end of turn". "Saddled is a designation that has no rules meaning other than to act as a marker ... Being saddled is not a part of the permanent's copiable values." Many creatures may crew one Vehicle; no persistent link.
13. **Merged components** (CR 731): not a relation between two permanents but fusion into one object (731.2, 731.3); see Q4.
14. **Meld** (CR 701.42): not checked in detail (referenced in 701.27h only).
Other candidates I did not check: reconfigure, bestow (it is "attached", rule 702.103 not read), "enchanted creature"-style Aura relations beyond 303.4, companion, banding (702.22; "banded with", mentioned in 508.1e, not read), Role (303.7 only 303.7a read), Saga chapters, Ring-bearer, monarch, initiative, day/night (not relations).
Kill test (idea 2): symmetric relations are rare — **pairing is the only one found, and it dissolves (unpairs) when either endpoint leaves (702.95e).** Attachment is many-to-one asymmetric and the host can have many. Blocking is the closest to a two-endpoint "active pair" but is per-combat only and persists as "blocked" after the blocker dies (509.1h).

## Q8. Tokens/copies off their zone; loops

**Verdict: yes — tokens and copies cease to exist as SBAs off the battlefield/stack; mandatory loops are draws; optional-action loops are not.**

CR 111.7: "A token that's in a zone other than the battlefield ceases to exist. This is a state-based action; see rule 704. (Note that if a token changes zones, applicable triggered abilities will trigger before the token ceases to exist.)"
CR 111.8: "A token that has left the battlefield can't move to another zone or come back onto the battlefield. If such a token would change zones, it remains in its current zone instead. It ceases to exist the next time state-based actions are checked; see rule 704."
CR 707.10a: "If a copy of a spell is in a zone other than the stack, it ceases to exist. If a copy of a card is in any zone other than the stack or the battlefield, it ceases to exist. These are state-based actions. See rule 704."
CR 707.10f: "Some effects copy a permanent spell. As that copy resolves, it ceases being a copy of a spell and becomes a token permanent. (See rule 608.3f.)"
CR 104.4b: "If a game that's not using the limited range of influence option (including a two-player game) somehow enters a "loop" of mandatory actions, repeating a sequence of events with no way to stop, the game is a draw. Loops that contain an optional action don't result in a draw."
CR 733.1b: "Occasionally the game gets into a state in which a set of actions could be repeated indefinitely (thus creating a "loop"). In that case, the shortcut rules can be used to determine how many times those actions are repeated without having to actually perform them, and how the loop is broken."
CR 733.2a: "At any point in the game, the player with priority may suggest a shortcut by describing a sequence of game choices, for all players, that may be legally taken based on the current game state and the predictable results of the sequence of choices. This sequence may be a non-repetitive series of choices, a loop that repeats a specified number of times, multiple loops, or nested loops, and may even cross multiple turns. It can't include conditional actions, where the outcome of a game event determines the next action a player takes. The ending point of this sequence must be a place where a player has priority, though it need not be the player proposing the shortcut. ..."
CR 733.3: "Sometimes a loop can be fragmented, meaning that each player involved in the loop performs an independent action that results in the same game state being reached multiple times. If that happens, the active player (or, if the active player is not involved in the loop, the first player in turn order who is involved) must then make a different game choice so the loop does not continue. ..."
CR 733.4: "If a loop contains only mandatory actions, the game is a draw. (See rules 104.4b and 104.4f.)"
CR 733.5: "No player can be forced to perform an action that would end a loop other than actions called for by objects involved in the loop. Example: A player controls Seal of Cleansing, an enchantment that reads, "Sacrifice Seal of Cleansing: Destroy target artifact or enchantment." A mandatory loop that involves an artifact begins. The player is not forced to sacrifice Seal of Cleansing to destroy the artifact and end the loop."
CR 733.6: "If a loop contains an effect that says "[A] unless [B]," where [A] and [B] are each actions, no player can be forced to perform [B] to break the loop. If no player chooses to perform [B], the loop will continue as though [A] were mandatory."
When a loop with choices is not a draw: 104.4b ("Loops that contain an optional action don't result in a draw"); and a shortcut cannot contain conditional actions (733.2a), so a nondeterministic/conditional computation is not a "loop" in the shortcut sense. 104.4f covers multiplayer limited-range-of-influence games (not read fully). Tournament Rules modify loop handling (733.1c: "Whenever the Tournament Rules contradict these rules during a tournament, the Tournament Rules take precedence."), which I did not read.
Relevance: a reduction that cycles forever via "may" copies avoids the 104.4b draw; a mandatory one (e.g. an unconditional trigger copying itself with no optional step) is a draw.
